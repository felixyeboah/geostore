Storefront, round two

Three new directions, built on a different premise from the first set. The earlier ones leaned on lifestyle photos with headlines and gradients laid over them, plus a lot of badges and purple. These don't. In all three, product images sit contained on flat neutral panels and text never sits on top of a photo — the pattern every genuinely clean retailer uses.

The photography was most of the problem. The images currently in apps/saas/modules/commerce/data/catalog.ts are moody and inconsistent — a purple neon gaming setup for Home tech, red-lit earbuds, wrist shots for the watches. No layout survives that. I screened about 50 candidates and these mockups use a consistent light, plain-background set instead. That swap alone accounts for a large share of the difference you're seeing.
Option D

Catalogue

Swiss, typographic, hairline

No hero image at all. A statement, a rule, a row of departments, then straight into the product grid. Near-monochrome, tight type, prices aligned to the right of each name.

  • The most restrained and the most confident
  • Fastest page — almost no decoration to load
  • Depends entirely on good copy and spacing
  • Least "shoppy" — may feel austere for a mass audience
Pick this if you want GeoStore to look like it has taste and nothing to prove.

Option E

Showroom

Soft neutral, generous, Apple-adjacent

Warm off-white background with large rounded panels. One featured product stage, four department cards, then a calm product grid. Roomy and premium without going dark or fussy.

  • Most immediately “expensive” looking
  • Friendliest of the three — still clearly retail
  • Big panels need decent images to fill them
  • Tallest page; more scrolling to reach products
Pick this if you want premium but still warm and easy for any shopper.
Open Option E
Option F

Counter

Structured, information-first

No hero. A filter rail on the left, products immediately on the right, each showing specs, stock and price. Organised like a shop that expects you to know what you came for.

  • Easiest to understand — nothing to decode
  • Scales best as the catalogue grows past 8 items
  • Filters are already real in your data model
  • Least atmospheric; more tool than showroom
Pick this if you'd rather people find and buy quickly than be impressed.
